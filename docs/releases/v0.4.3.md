# Utterform 0.4.3 — The start sound, on every speaker

When you dictate with the window hidden, the click that says *recording* is all you get. On a Bluetooth headset it was often missing. This release fixes that, and the same fix covers every other output that is slow to start.

## What went wrong

An output device that has nothing to play suspends. AirPods do, and so do HDMI displays, docks and most USB headsets. Waking one takes a few hundred milliseconds before it carries any sound at all, and once awake it queues 150–250 ms more.

Utterform gave the cue 500 ms and then closed the stream. A closed stream is discarded, not drained. So on a sleeping speaker the click was written into a device that was still waking up and thrown away before it could be heard — while the same speaker played the stop click perfectly, because by then it was awake.

Worse, the failure was invisible. The cue's result was thrown away at the call site, so a click nobody heard looked exactly like a click that played.

## What changed

**The speaker is woken first.** Pressing the dictation key now opens the output stream before the microphone, so the wake-up overlaps with opening the microphone instead of queueing behind it. The cue sounds as early as the hardware allows.

**The cue is played to the end.** Every cue now carries 300 ms of silence after the tone, and the stream stays open until the device has taken all of it. The 500 ms cut-off became a three-second safety net rather than the normal case — an awake speaker still finishes in a fraction of that.

**A cue opens with silence.** 60 ms of it, so a device that discards the first samples it is handed discards silence rather than the tone.

**The recording waits for the cue instead of the cue waiting for the recording.** The microphone opens immediately and discards until the cue has been played, then keeps everything. The click now means what you assume it means — *from here, what you say is recorded* — and it cannot end up inside its own recording. The ten-minute limit counts from there too.

**A cue that cannot play says so.** Failures are reported instead of dropped, including the case that used to look like success: an output device whose sample format Utterform cannot write. If the start sound cannot be played at all, a desktop notification stands in for it, because with the window hidden you would otherwise have nothing.

**The stop click no longer delays your text.** It plays in full on its own, while transcription starts.

None of this is platform-specific. Linux, macOS and Windows run the same code and all three get the same behaviour.

## Upgrading

Nothing to do and nothing to configure. **Settings → Recording feedback** still switches the sounds off, and the cues sound as they always did.
